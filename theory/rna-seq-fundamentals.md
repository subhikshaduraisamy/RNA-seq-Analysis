# A Primer on RNA-sequencing


## The RNA in the RNA-seq

As an intermediate in the central dogma, RNA is very well known as *"messenger ribonucleic acid [mRNA] intermediate"* transcribed from DNA as the template. 



But mRNA is not the only RNA in the biological system. While the coding regions of the genome roughly constitute 2% of the entire genome, the 75 - 90% of the non-coding regions code for thousands of unique non-coding RNAs [ncRNAs]. ncRNAs range from rRNAs, tRNAs, miRNAs, piRNAs, snRNAs, snoRNAs and many more fine tuning the regulation of gene expression at different levels of the central dogma. While sequencing the mRNAs are crucial to  understand the transcriptome, it is equally important to understand the regulatory layer of gene expression through sequencing the non-coding RNAs.


## Why sequence the RNA?

Sequencing literally translates into identifying the exact order of the nitrogenous bases, in context of nucleic acid. When the sequence of the RNA is going to be exactly complementary to the DNA template with Thymine replaced by Uracil, why do we sequence them? 



RNA-sequencing has multi-dimensional components to it - one, is to determine the exact sequence of the nitrogenous bases in the sample and two, is to quantify the exact expression - in terms of where (spatial), when (temporal), and how much (differentially expressed genes) RNA is being transcribed. This aids in understanding the gene expression patterns, post-transcriptional modifications, and changes in different conditions with respect to a control sample expression.
